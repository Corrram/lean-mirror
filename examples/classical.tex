\section*{LeanMirror: classical mathematics}
The following statements are interpreted in Lean and proved equivalent to the indicated mathlib theorem specialized to real numbers.

\subsection*{Triangle inequality}
\begin{statement}
Let $x$ be a real number. Let $y$ be a real number. Then
\[
\left|\left(x + y\right)\right| \le \left(\left|x\right| + \left|y\right|\right).
\]
\end{statement}
Mathlib counterpart: \texttt{abs\_add\_le}.

\subsection*{Absolute value of a product}
\begin{statement}
Let $x$ be a real number. Let $y$ be a real number. Then
\[
\left|x\,y\right| = \left|x\right|\,\left|y\right|.
\]
\end{statement}
Mathlib counterpart: \texttt{abs\_mul}.

\subsection*{Quadratic arithmetic--geometric mean inequality}
\begin{statement}
Let $x$ be a real number. Let $y$ be a real number. Then
\[
2\,x\,y \le \left(x^{2} + y^{2}\right).
\]
\end{statement}
Mathlib counterpart: \texttt{two\_mul\_le\_add\_sq}.
\paragraph{Definitions and scope.} All variables are real numbers; $x^2$ denotes $x\cdot x$. Absolute value, addition, multiplication, and order have their usual real-number interpretations in mathlib. The quadratic AM--GM form applies to all real $x,y$, with no nonnegativity hypothesis.
\paragraph{What is checked.} Lean checks each statement equivalence and the resulting proof. The equivalences unfold definitions; they do not invoke the three inequalities or identities being transferred. The display formulas are generated from those same typed statements. The parser, notation, and renderer remain an explicit, unverified presentation boundary.
